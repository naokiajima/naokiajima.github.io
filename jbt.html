01 — Background

About the client

Japanese Body Therapy is a Japanese-style chiropractic clinic in Singapore, founded by Kizaki Yuta — who comes from a background of managing over 187 osteopathic clinics across Japan. Their method, PM Balance Seitai, targets posture correction, pelvic realignment, and deep muscle adjustment to restore the body's natural structure.

This client came through my former company. Their previous website had become inaccessible, so they needed a full rebuild from scratch — logo, color system, content structure, and WordPress development. The primary orange was chosen by the client to match their physical store's existing branding.

02 — Problem & Goal

What needed to be solved

Starting from zero meant no templates, no content structure, no established brand online. At the same time, the client had a clear idea of what he wanted displayed — which sometimes pulled against my instinct for simplicity.

Challenge

No website, strong opinions

Build from scratch. Client had specific requirements on content. Two emerging audiences — treatment seekers and franchise investors — with no clear separation.

Goal

Trust, clarity, bookings

Build enough trust in the first scroll to convert visitors. Surface the right treatment information. Use social proof — reviews and before/afters — to do the convincing.

The audience tension

The site was originally built for one audience — people seeking posture treatment. With the later addition of a franchise page, the same site now needs to speak to investors too. These two groups want very different things.

Treatment seekers Franchise investors
What they want Proof treatment works, real results, easy booking Business model, ROI, brand credibility
What they see Relevant Mixed signals
Ideal fix Optimise current structure Separate landing page or clear nav split

03 — Design Decisions

Homepage anatomy

My default approach is always simplicity — set the target audience first, then build the structure around what they need to see. Here, the client came with content already in mind. So rather than fight it, I focused on sequencing: making sure each section builds logically on the one before, guiding a skeptical visitor toward confidence and action.

  • 01
    Hero banner
    Immediately filters the right visitor — "if you're looking for posture correction in Singapore, you're in the right place." Reduces bounce from irrelevant traffic and sets the tone in under 3 seconds.
    ↳ Audience definition
  • 02
    Treatment video
    Shows the therapy in action. For a body clinic, seeing is believing — it reduces the fear of the unknown and makes the first visit feel less intimidating.
    ↳ Reduce friction
  • 03
    Conditions list
    Speaks directly to the visitor's pain — neck pain, back pain, pelvic issues. The moment someone sees their condition listed, they keep reading. Increases scroll depth significantly.
    ↳ Problem-solution match
  • 04
    Before / After gallery
    Visual proof of real, measurable results. Originally far larger — negotiated down to 6 images to protect page speed without losing impact. (See Challenges section.)
    ↳ Visual social proof
  • 05
    Certified professionals
    A short credibility section — founder background, 187+ clinics in Japan. Answers "can I trust these people?" before the visitor has to ask.
    ↳ Authority & credibility
  • 06
    Who it's for
    Desk workers, postpartum mothers, chronic pain sufferers. A second point of identification — if they missed themselves in the conditions list, they might find themselves here.
    ↳ Audience segmentation
  • 07
    Full review section
    A wall of real, named customer reviews in both English and Japanese. The sheer volume becomes a signal — this many people can't all be wrong. Closes the page with strong conviction.
    ↳ Volume social proof

04 — Visual Design

Color & branding

The primary orange came directly from the client — it mirrors the physical store's color scheme, so the website feels like a natural extension of the in-person experience. The rest of the palette stays neutral to let the orange carry the brand weight without competing.

05 — Challenges

What went wrong, and how I handled it

Challenge 01

Content overload

The client initially wanted a very large before/after gallery — as long as the full review section. This caused serious page load issues and hurt the scroll experience before visitors even reached the reviews.

Resolution

Negotiated to 6 images

Communicated the loading speed impact to the consultant, who relayed it to the client. Reduced to 6 high-quality images — enough visual proof without killing performance.

Challenge 02

Client-directed structure

The client had strong opinions on what to display and how. This constrained my ability to pursue a simpler, more conversion-focused layout.

Resolution

Work within the brief

Accepted the content requirements and focused on sequencing, spacing, and visual hierarchy — making the existing content as clean and readable as possible.

06 — Reflection

What I think about this website

"I honestly want to revamp the whole thing. With the franchise page added, we now have two target audiences — and the same content doesn't serve both. The fundamental question the site can't currently answer is: who is this actually for?"

What worked

  • ✓ Hero clearly defines the visitor
  • ✓ Review volume builds real credibility
  • ✓ Before/after is convincing at 6 images
  • ✓ Bilingual (EN / JP) serves the market
  • ✓ Orange branding matches physical store

What I'd change

  • → Separate patient and franchise journeys
  • → Reduce homepage content density
  • → Add a booking CTA above the fold
  • → Better image optimisation for speed
  • → Clearer information architecture overall
