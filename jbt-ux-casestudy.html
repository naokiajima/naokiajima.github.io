Background

Why I'm proposing this

I designed and built the JBT website from scratch — logo, color system, content architecture, and WordPress development. The site serves a wellness clinic offering posture correction and scoliosis-related therapy in Singapore.

As the business evolved, a franchise section was added to the same homepage. What started as a single-audience site now tries to speak to treatment-seeking patients and franchise investors simultaneously — without any structural separation. The result is a homepage that is content-heavy, hard to scan, and unclear about who it is actually for.

"I honestly want to revamp the whole thing. With the franchise page added, we now have two target audiences. Who is the main target audience on this website?"

This proposal presents a full redesign of the homepage structure — grounded in UX objectives defined for the primary audience: busy working adults dealing with posture or back pain.

Primary Audience

Who we're designing for

All design decisions in this proposal are anchored to one primary user type: the working adult with posture or back pain, encountering JBT for the first time.

Profile Working adults or seniors
Problem Posture pain, back pain, neck stiffness — often chronic and worsening
Behaviour Scans rather than reads. Looks for proof. Needs reassurance before booking.
Barrier Fear of the unknown. Unsure if this therapy is right for them. Needs a clear next step.
UX Objectives

What this redesign must achieve

Clarify what the therapy is within the first screen
Separate informational reassurance from conversion moments
Reduce excessive scrolling fatigue
Improve page performance perception
Create progressive disclosure of trust signals
Guide users through a logical decision journey
Navigation to remain simple and addresses their needs

02 — Proposed Structure

Redesigned homepage flow

The redesigned structure is built around one principle: each section has a single job, and the order follows the user's natural decision journey — from "is this for me?" to "I'm ready to book."

Expand each section to see the wireframe, rationale, and what changes from the current site.

1
Hero Section
Purpose: Immediate relevance confirmation
Clarity ▾
Content structure
Headline: "Relieve Posture Pain with Japanese Body Therapy"
Subtext: Specialised posture correction and body alignment therapy in Singapore
Primary CTA: Book Consultation
Secondary CTA: Learn How It Works

The hero has one job: confirm within 3 seconds that this service is for people with posture or back pain. The headline states the problem and the solution. The two CTAs serve two mindsets — the visitor who is ready to book, and the visitor who needs more information first. Neither gets left behind.

↑

Currently, the first booking CTA appears after 30+ reviews — well below the fold. This brings it to screen one.

2
Conditions Treated
Purpose: Quick self-identification
Scannable ▾
Content structure
Visual icon grid — 6 to 8 conditions maximum
Back pain · Neck stiffness · Pelvic imbalance · Posture correction
Shoulder tension · Headaches · Postpartum recovery · Scoliosis support
Each tile: icon + short label only. No descriptions at this stage.

The current version lists conditions as a dense text block. A user with neck pain scanning a 12-item paragraph has to work to find themselves in it. A visual grid lets the same user confirm relevance in under 5 seconds — which is the threshold before bounce.

↑

Replaces the long plain-text conditions list with a scannable icon grid. Faster to read, and easier to self-identify.

3
How Treatment Works
Purpose: Reduce fear of the unknown
Reassurance ▾
Content structure
Step 1 — Consultation & assessment: We identify your posture imbalances
Step 2 — Body alignment therapy: Targeted correction using PM Balance Seitai
Step 3 — Follow-up & improvement tracking: Progress monitored each session

Many first-time visitors have never tried chiropractic or body therapy. They don't know what will happen when they walk in. This section removes that uncertainty by showing a simple, predictable 3-step journey. In wellness UX, reducing fear of the unknown is one of the highest-impact conversion levers available.

This section does not currently exist on the homepage.

↑

New section. Directly addresses "what actually happens when I go there?" — one of the most common hesitations before a first wellness booking.

4
Before & After Results
Purpose: Confidence without performance penalty
Proof ▾
Content structure
3 transformation pairs shown by default — lazy loaded
Each pair: Condition treated + number of sessions as caption
"View more results" — expands inline without page reload

Visual proof of results is essential in wellness UX — it converts where text cannot. The current gallery loads all images simultaneously, which directly impacts perceived page speed. Displaying 3 compelling examples by default with progressive reveal keeps the proof front and centre without the performance cost.

Adding a caption — condition and session count — makes each example immediately meaningful. It answers: "Has this worked for someone like me?"

↑

Reduces initial image load significantly. The proof is still present — it is just not frontloaded all at once.

5
Therapist Credibility
Purpose: Build emotional trust
Trust ▾
Content structure
Therapist photo — real, human, not stock
Name, specialisation, years of experience
Founder background: 187+ clinics in Japan
Certifications or training — concise, credible, not a full CV

In wellness UX, people need to trust the practitioner before they trust the treatment. A real face, a real name, and a clear credential does far more than a descriptive paragraph. This section must feel human — not a corporate "about us" block. It is the emotional anchor before the user reaches the booking CTA.

Currently this information appears as a short text block with no face attached to it. Putting a face to the expertise changes the emotional quality of the trust signal significantly.

6
Customer Testimonials
Purpose: Emotional reassurance — without overwhelm
Social proof ▾
Content structure
Average star rating — prominently displayed (e.g. 4.9 / 5 · 80+ reviews)
3–4 selected testimonials — chosen for emotional range and specificity
Each review: name, condition, outcome — short and scannable
"Read all reviews" — expandable inline, not a new page

Displaying 30+ reviews in full is counterproductive. It overwhelms the reader, pushes the conversion CTA far down the page, and creates scroll fatigue right before the moment of action. A prominently displayed average rating communicates the same confidence signal immediately.

Selected testimonials provide the emotional specificity — "this worked for someone with my exact problem." The expandable section keeps the full list accessible for visitors who want depth, without forcing it on everyone.

↑

Reduces review section from 30+ full entries to a star rating + 4 selected reviews. The trust signal is stronger, and the conversion CTA is no longer buried beneath it.

7
Booking CTA Section
Purpose: Natural, friction-free action trigger
Conversion ▾
Content structure
Primary CTA: Book Appointment — prominent, unmissable
Secondary options: Call us · WhatsApp us
Location — Suntec City Mall, Singapore
Reception hours — 10am to 8pm

By this point the user has confirmed the therapy is for them, understands what happens in a session, seen real results, and trusts the practitioner. The booking section should feel like the natural next step — not a hard sell. The combination of a booking button, phone, and WhatsApp accommodates different communication preferences, which is especially important for a Singapore audience where WhatsApp is a primary contact channel.

8
Franchise Section
Purpose: Serve secondary audience without disrupting primary journey
IA fix ▾
Content structure
Position: Near footer, clearly separated from treatment content
Short introduction: "Interested in opening a JBT clinic?"
Single CTA: Learn about franchise opportunities → (links to dedicated page)

The overwhelming majority of homepage visitors are patients, not investors. Franchise content placed in the main page flow creates confusion about who the site is for — a patient reading about treatment should not encounter investor acquisition copy mid-journey.

Moving the franchise section near the footer keeps it discoverable for the right audience without disrupting the treatment user's decision journey. A dedicated franchise landing page is the proper long-term solution.

↑

Resolves the core audience confusion. Treatment visitors no longer scroll past investor content to find their booking CTA.

UX Principles Applied

The design rationale

Each structural decision in this proposal maps back to an established UX principle. These are not aesthetic preferences — they are behavioural patterns that affect whether users stay, trust, and convert.

Progressive disclosure
Information is revealed in layers — overview first, detail on demand. Reduces overwhelm on first visit.
Cognitive load reduction
Scannable grids replace walls of text. Fewer decisions per screen. Clearer visual hierarchy throughout.
Trust sequencing
Trust signals are introduced progressively — process first, proof second, credentials third — mirroring how people actually decide.
Audience segmentation
Primary and secondary audiences are clearly separated. Each gets their own entry point and journey, not a shared page that serves neither well.
Conversion clarity
The booking CTA is present at the top and bottom of the page. The user always knows what the next step is, wherever they are in the scroll.
Performance perception
Lazy loading and progressive image reveal reduce perceived load time, even before optimising file sizes. First impression matters.
